HOW BIG IS THE CIRCLE?

THE GUT RULE

Every variable does one of two jobs. m and v decide how hard the charge is to turn: they keep it straight. q and B decide how hard the field shoves it sideways: they turn it.

More "keeps it straight" → wider circle. More "turns it" → tighter circle.

m ↑Heavier is harder to turn → wider.
v ↑Faster is harder to turn → wider.
q ↑More charge, the field grips it harder → tighter.
B ↑Stronger field, harder sideways shove → tighter.

THE MODEL: A TUG OF WAR

To go round a circle of radius r, the charge needs a push toward the centre of mv²/r. The field supplies qvB. The circle settles at the size where the two are equal. Change a variable and one side wins for a moment, so the circle grows or shrinks until the fight is even again.

THE EXACT MATHS

qvB = mv²r

Cancel one v from both sides and rearrange for r:

r = mvqB

COMMON TRAPS

q is on the bottom. Double the charge and the radius halves, not doubles.

Speed is sneaky. Doubling v doubles the shove (qvB), but the push needed (mv²/r) goes up 4 times. So the circle still doubles.

The field never speeds it up. The shove is always at 90° to the motion, so it does no work. Speed stays the same; only the direction changes.
